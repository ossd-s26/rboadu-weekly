## Weekly Post 
Reading The Cathedral and the Bazaar changed how I conceptualize software development. The cathedral model feels more structured and more controlled, but less iterative and slower. I imagine a small team of software engineers meticulously constructing something behind closed doors, while the bazaar model feels chaotic but more constructive with tons of collaboration, rapid iterations, and quick responsive feedback. I find the bazaar model more exciting and more realistic than the cathedral model for modern software development. I think the bazaar model captures more of how collaboration occurs on the internet through open-source software development where contributors from all walks of life and different domains contribute through discussions and improvements in real-time.

That said, varying circumstances merit each model. The cathedral model is more useful in more sensitive and more critical contexts where stability, security, and consistency are more critical. However, I value the bazaar model more because I value learning, openness, and community growth, which I value more learning, openness, and community growth. These are the things assisted programming tools are likely to promote. In some way, AI programming tools seem to blur the lines. They seem to promote the bazaar model, as with AI, contributing to those models is much easier. Someone can generate code, iterate and test their ideas, and even contribute to open-source projects without going through the learning curve as deep programming knowledge is not needed to start. On the contrary, should large tech companies be the only ones to control the more advanced programming tools, they could promote the cathedral model by being the only ones with the type of power to centralize the programming community. AI raises several concerns from the standpoint of software development. 

For me Artificial Intelligence is a tool that can enhance both models. It works best when paired with the collaborative spirit of the bazaar. Artificial Intelligence lowers the barrier to entry. It should not replace the human insight, discussion and iteration that make open source so effective.

In terms of our group project we have made progress so far especially on the frontend. We have started building out components and structuring the user interface, which is helping us better understand how everything connects. Personally I have been working on integrating parts of the user interface and thinking about how user interactions should flow. There were definitely moments of confusion. When trying to connect components or understand how everything fits together. But I feel like things are starting to click more now. I want us to focus on making the application more functional and polished. That includes connecting the frontend to the backend making sure data flows correctly and improving the user experience. I also think we should spend more time testing and debugging since that is where a lot of issues tend to come up. With a few weeks left, staying organized and dividing tasks clearly will be really important.

From reading groups progress reports it seems like many teams are facing similar challenges. Especially with integration and managing time. Some groups are along, in backend development while others are still refining their frontend. It is interesting to see how different teams prioritize parts of the project and how Artificial Intelligence is being used by these teams to enhance their models.
